# Lebedev Web Design

Take creative and practical ownership of the whole website: its purpose, positioning, structure, original content, visual identity, user journeys, functionality, conversion, discoverability, implementation, and operation. Treat an existing site as evidence and raw material. A broad brief may call for an entirely new concept, narrative, architecture, and experience; do not default to reskinning the current pages or waiting for the user to supply finished copy and images.

Produce original work informed by the studio's combination of inventive expression, research, technical craft, and attention to the task. The result may be exuberant, restrained, richly illustrated, or information-dense. A dramatic hero, minimal palette, single metaphor, or oversized headline is an option, not a signature that every project must wear.

This is an independent interpretation. Distinguish the studio's published project accounts, Artemy Lebedev's dated essays, and this skill's contemporary implementation recommendations. Attribute team projects to their credited authors. Do not confuse Art. Lebedev Studio with the separate designer at artemiilebedev.com.

## Understand the whole project

Inspect the brief, business or institution, audience, offer, existing site and implementation, available analytics, real content, assets, language, and constraints. Identify what visitors need to understand or accomplish and what is missing or wrong. A successful result may mean finding a document, comparing specifications, feeling the character of a place, or completing a purchase; it need not be a single conversion action. Use available connected sources before asking for facts. Make reasonable creative decisions and ask only when missing information materially changes the result.

For broad creation, improvement, or management briefs, choose what to retain, rewrite, remove, invent, or rebuild based on the task. Existing copy, page order, imagery, and styling are not automatically requirements. Consider the whole website even when starting from one page. Respect explicit limits such as "only fix the header" or "keep this identity"; creative freedom does not cancel those instructions or authorize unrelated operational changes.

Read [website-workflow.md](references/website-workflow.md) for strategy, original content and asset production, journeys, conversion, search, implementation, and ongoing management. Use the parts that affect this task; a small fix does not need a full discovery exercise.

## Choose and verify relevant evidence

Read [design-research.md](references/design-research.md) to select relevant studio examples. Match references to the task, not merely the client's industry. Examine client designs inside case studies separately from the studio portfolio's surrounding UI. If the user explicitly wants the studio's own website appearance, inspect that requested surface directly instead of substituting a client-project style.

For substantial new or reimagined projects, and whenever current or latest studio work is requested, perform that reference's freshness check before settling the direction. Consult the official Russian sources alongside available English versions. Reuse evidence already verified for the current project unless the brief, source, or relevant work has changed; do not restart research for every small edit. Older work remains useful when its reasoning fits better.

Visually inspect the references that materially determine composition, imagery, or interaction. Record the source, review date, surface, and what was actually observed. Distinguish a case-study image, an embedded demonstration, and a live client journey; a screenshot does not verify responsiveness or behavior. State access gaps and continue with supportable evidence rather than presenting text descriptions as visual verification.

Use [mandership-guide.md](references/mandership-guide.md) for reasoning about typography, information, navigation, constraints, composition, and originality. Read only the pertinent sections. The essays contain strong opinions, exceptions, and revisions: don't convert an isolated statement into a universal design law or current engineering requirement.

Choose the appropriate emphasis; a single site may need several:

- **New project or reinvention:** originate the proposition, site structure, narrative, media, and experience from the visitor's needs and the real offer.
- **Presentation or discovery:** develop a memorable premise, atmosphere, image, editorial treatment, or interaction around the subject and audience.
- **Information or repeat use:** make structure, navigation, hierarchy, comparisons, and meaningful detail carry the design. Express identity without obstructing frequent tasks.
- **Commerce or service:** connect convincing presentation with useful selection, pricing, configuration, forms, and accurate states.
- **Management or optimization:** diagnose content, journey, measurement, search, and operational problems; implement the improvements supported by the brief and evidence.
- **Explicitly scoped refinement:** improve the requested component or journey within its stated boundaries. A new site concept is optional, not a prerequisite.

## Develop and test the direction

Define the task, audience, proposition, important journeys, content and functionality, creative premise, and how success will be checked. Keep this brief and continue into the requested deliverable. An implementation request calls for a working result, not just recommendations or a mood board.

For substantial new directions, explore genuinely different strategies, narratives, compositions, or interactions before committing; a rough page with original copy or a small prototype can reveal more than adjectives. Scale exploration to the task rather than requiring a fixed number of alternatives. Honor the user's stated choices while questioning inherited assumptions. Revise a weak first idea instead of polishing it indefinitely, and do not impose repeated approvals for routine reversible decisions.

Write representative, factually grounded content and produce the assets needed for an important page, complex detail view, or component. Arrange and evaluate them, then derive a grid, spacing relationships, and reusable styles. Let the existing design system guide an explicitly scoped edit; establish a new system when the broader project calls for one. Prove the direction beyond a single hero, including relevant internal pages, journeys, and content states.

## Create the content and experience

Write original positioning, page narratives, headings, explanations, calls to action, product or service descriptions, FAQs, and interface messages as needed. Reorganize or replace weak inherited copy. Ground factual claims in the real offering and available evidence; distinguish proposed offers or missing business facts from promises the business actually makes.

Originate and produce the visual assets the concept needs using available image-generation, illustration, photography, diagramming, lettering, 3D, or motion tools. Give assets a coherent purpose, composition, crop, and relationship to the copy. Use existing assets when they work; do not settle for generic placeholders when original production is available. Generated imagery must not misrepresent actual products, premises, people, or results.

Design the useful functionality as part of the concept: navigation, search, comparison, configuration, forms, booking, commerce, accounts, or editorial tools where the task warrants them. Connect content and interactions into complete journeys. Improve conversion through clarity, credible evidence, relevant offers, and reduced friction; select outcomes and measurement appropriate to the site's purpose. Use [website-workflow.md](references/website-workflow.md) for the details.

## Apply the art direction

**Give the design a reason.** A product mechanism, cultural context, visitor concern, brand association, or information structure can supply the idea. Literal product imagery is only one route. A metaphor, expressive gesture, pattern, or joke can create recognition and emotion without explaining a feature. Judge whether it serves this brief. Avoid interchangeable decoration, but don't mistake ornament or pleasure for a defect.

**Make hierarchy deliberate.** Use size, weight, placement, color, imagery, and spacing to establish relationships. Keep headings with their sections and labels with their values. Use density and empty space where they help reading or emphasis. Asymmetry, repetition, symmetry, and cards are all available; select and refine them for the material instead of adopting or banning them by fashion.

**Treat typography as content design.** Choose typefaces for character, reading conditions, language coverage, and licensing. There is no fixed font count or compulsory display face. Review actual line breaks, tracking, leading, punctuation, captions, numbers, and emphasis. Consider optical alignment and hanging punctuation where they improve a stable composition, without brittle fixes that fail on reflow. Keep content readable and editable; never use an image of an entire page as the implementation.

**Choose color and media intentionally.** A quiet scheme or many vivid colors may both be appropriate. Define their roles and visual relationships, then test the composition. Use photography, illustration, diagrams, lettering, or 3D when they contribute to the intended experience. Motion can be central to a concept: prototype it and plan its loading, touch behavior, and equivalent reduced-motion experience rather than reflexively removing it. Commission or generate original assets within scope, or identify missing assets honestly. Use licensed material; do not reuse client screenshots or proprietary studio assets as the new site's branding, or imply studio affiliation.

**Make navigation intelligible.** Visitors should understand their current location, available choices, and likely destinations. Use meaningful text labels where an icon would be ambiguous, and descriptive links whose surrounding sentences still make sense. Novel presentation may be appropriate; consistent behavior and visible feedback remain essential. Keep content and actions available on keyboard, touch, and narrow screens. Preserve the useful meaning of an interaction when motion is reduced; sound begins with a user action.

**Edit words as carefully as pictures.** Invent the expression and narrative; verify the facts. Choose detail appropriate to the reading situation. A short slogan and a long technical explanation can each be right. Humor, informal typography, and surprise are expressive tools, not compulsory tone. Profanity and the designer's personal views are not prerequisites. Do not fabricate claims, testimonials, prices, or capabilities. Raise material contradictions in supplied facts instead of silently rewriting them. Add a news feed only when useful current content and maintenance exist; retain dates when currency matters.

## Implement, operate, and finish

Read [implementation-review.md](references/implementation-review.md) for the checks appropriate to the work. Use the requested platform and tooling. For a rebuild, deliberately map or migrate routes, content, data, validation, submission destinations, checkout, and analytics; these need continuity even when the experience changes completely. For local edits, preserve behavior outside scope and scope styles. For CMS work, keep content editable and honor draft/publication status.

Evaluate the actual result against the task and intended character: do the proposition, content, assets, and functionality work together, does the important journey work, and does the design feel deliberately composed? Review relevant pages and states at desktop and narrow widths. Fix failures rather than declaring success from compilation alone. Follow applicable versioning, changelog, build, and deployment instructions within the user's authorization. For ongoing work, use observed outcomes to prioritize the next improvement; do not claim conversion gains without measurements.

Deliver the artifact or implemented change with a short rationale, the checks actually performed, and specific remaining limitations. Keep research citations in the handoff rather than the customer's UI unless requested. Distinguish a concept, local implementation, validated integration, and published result.
