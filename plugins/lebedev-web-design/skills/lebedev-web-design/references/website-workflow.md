# Own the whole website

This is the skill's working method for complete website projects and ongoing management. It combines the studio-informed reasoning in [design-research.md](design-research.md) with practical website work; it is not an official Art. Lebedev Studio process. Use the relevant parts rather than requiring every project to produce every document or feature.

## Establish what the website should achieve

Understand the organization, offering, audiences, language, geography, acquisition channels, business model, and operating capacity. Use the brief, existing content, product evidence, connected sources, search results, and available analytics. Separate known facts from hypotheses and proposed changes. Inspect actual pages and journeys, not just the homepage screenshot.

Identify visitors' entry conditions, questions, concerns, decisions, and next steps. A person arriving from an advertisement, a returning account user, and a researcher may need different paths. Define success in terms appropriate to the site: qualified inquiries, completed purchases, activation, repeat use, successful information retrieval, or a convincing cultural or editorial experience. Do not invent a sales funnel for every institution or publication.

For an open brief, recommend and execute a coherent direction. Write a short working brief: audience and need, proposition, important journeys, content and functionality, creative premise, constraints, and verification. Make assumptions visible without making the user answer a questionnaire. Ask for a missing factual or business decision only when it materially affects what can honestly be built or promised.

## Reconsider the site from the ground up

Treat the current sitemap, copy, visual identity, templates, and interactions as hypotheses. Decide which deserve to survive. A strong solution may need a different narrative, page structure, naming system, format, asset set, or flow, including an entirely new website concept. Propose changes to the underlying offer when useful, but distinguish proposals from approved prices, guarantees, services, or commercial commitments.

Preserve explicit user constraints and dependencies that still serve the project. Do not assume that an existing brand must change, or that it must remain unchanged. When the brief permits reinvention, judge each choice on its merits. An explicit request to fix one defect still receives a focused implementation, with broader findings explained only when useful.

Choose alternatives at the level where they differ meaningfully: audience framing, value proposition, editorial narrative, information architecture, interaction model, or art direction. Comparing only colors is insufficient if the problem is unclear positioning. Explore with representative copy, imagery, and a real journey; select and refine rather than stopping at a list of ideas.

## Build the architecture and journeys

Design the sitemap, page purposes, navigation labels, internal links, and content model together. Decide what belongs on a landing page, an explanatory page, a catalog, a detail page, a comparison, an account area, or a maintained editorial section. Add, combine, reorder, and remove pages when the project warrants it. Avoid default section stacks that have no relationship to visitors' questions.

Map the path from entry to a useful result and the relevant return paths. Design supporting states such as unavailable products, empty results, validation errors, confirmation, cancellation, recovery, and returning visitors. Determine which functions require real data, a backend, a CMS, or an external service. A visual concept may show a proposed feature if clearly labeled; an implementation must connect it or report the missing integration.

When replacing an existing structure, inventory valuable URLs and inbound links, content ownership, data sources, forms, transactions, and measurement before migration. Maintain or deliberately replace their contracts; freedom to reinvent the experience is compatible with protecting users' data and completed work.

## Write original copy

Develop the message hierarchy and voice from the audience, real offer, and creative premise. Write the actual content: proposition, headlines, explanations, service and product descriptions, comparisons, objections, FAQs, calls to action, navigation, form help, validation, confirmations, metadata, and accessibility text as needed. A broad creation brief includes authorship; do not wait for supplied final copy or merely rearrange existing text.

Use concrete information to make the offer understandable. Decide which claims require evidence, what readers need before acting, and how much detail each situation supports. Replace generic slogans with relevant meaning. Original phrasing is free to be inventive; facts, quotations, testimonials, credentials, specifications, results, scarcity, and prices require support. If proof is unavailable, choose another honest argument or mark a factual gap in the handoff rather than manufacturing proof for the layout.

Compose and revise copy in its actual layout. Test long titles, realistic product names, numbers, translations, and mobile line breaks. Preserve meaning when localizing while adapting rhythm and idiom. Keep search intent, accessibility, and plain navigation labels compatible with the site's voice.

## Produce original media

Determine what each asset communicates and whether photography, illustration, a diagram, lettering, a product demonstration, 3D, motion, or an interactive tool is the strongest format. Create a coherent visual vocabulary tied to the subject. Produce needed assets with available tools instead of stopping at descriptions of images someone else should make.

For generated or commissioned assets, specify the subject, message, composition, palette, material, lighting, crop, text-safe area, and responsive variants that matter. Review the output in context, revise weak results, optimize delivery, and add appropriate alternative text. Keep important copy as editable text where possible. Prototype motion with loading and reduced-motion behavior in mind.

Distinguish illustrative scenes from evidence of actual products, premises, personnel, or results. Use supplied, licensed, or original material; do not borrow studio client assets as the new brand. If a tool or required real-world asset is unavailable, deliver what can be completed and state the precise remaining asset requirement.

## Improve conversion and measure outcomes

For a commercial site, examine the whole decision: acquisition intent, relevance of the promise, understanding of the offer, supporting proof, selection, pricing, action, completion, and subsequent use. Improve the point that is actually limiting the journey. Useful changes can include better explanations, comparisons, demonstrations, visible costs, fewer unnecessary fields, clearer delivery terms, accessible controls, or a more appropriate call to action.

Use available analytics and qualitative evidence to identify friction. Verify event definitions before interpreting a funnel: a button click is different from a successfully delivered lead or paid order. Distinguish total submissions from qualified outcomes, and account for device, traffic source, consent, spam, and measurement gaps where they affect the conclusion.

When evidence is limited, state a reasoned hypothesis and a practical way to evaluate it; do not promise an uplift. If an experiment is appropriate and authorized, define its audience, changed behavior, primary outcome, guardrails, and evaluation window before launch. Use a controlled comparison when feasible; treat uncontrolled before/after results cautiously. Never create fake urgency, hidden charges, or misleading proof to increase a metric.

## Make the site discoverable and maintainable

Organize useful content around actual visitor questions and search intent. Plan descriptive titles, headings, URLs, internal links, metadata, and appropriate structured data around real page content. Handle indexing, canonical URLs, redirects, and sitemaps according to the environment; keep staging and publication status intentional. Browse current primary documentation when technical or platform-specific requirements are consequential. Avoid speculative search guarantees or mass-produced pages without distinct value.

Choose implementation and content editing arrangements the owner can maintain. Account for performance, accessibility, mobile and RTL behavior, data handling, integrations, and routine content updates. Follow the requested platform; do not introduce a new stack, paid service, or infrastructure merely to express a visual concept.

For ongoing management, work from the observed issue or agreed objective. Review relevant content currency, broken journeys, analytics, search visibility, asset delivery, dependencies, and publishing workflows. Prioritize changes by user value, evidence, effort, and operational consequence. Keep useful backups or rollback paths for meaningful migrations and releases. Ongoing responsibility does not automatically create scheduled jobs, monitoring subscriptions, or spending commitments.

## Deliver a working outcome

Carry the selected direction into the requested artifact or implementation, including original content and assets. Use [implementation-review.md](implementation-review.md) to verify the real journey, rendered pages, and affected integrations. Follow project versioning, changelog, build, and deployment instructions. Publication and consequential business actions follow the user's authorization; complete all independently useful preparation first.

Report the resulting concept and behavior, what was created or changed, what was checked, the actual deployment state, and material remaining dependencies. Keep the handoff short. Distinguish an attractive prototype, a connected implementation, and demonstrated business outcomes.
